The Josef Mayer Research Project

Sources, evidence & research notes

A transparent guide to the documents behind the Josef Mayer research project.

This page separates original documents, personal recollections and later historical interpretations. Dates and quotations are attributed to the evidence available; unresolved points are identified rather than filled with assumptions.

Source hierarchy. Patents establish the content and dates of filed technical claims. Autobiographical testimony records Mayer’s own account of his life, not independent verification of every event. Historic photographs document visible subjects but require independent evidence for dates, places and identities. Later publications provide interpretation and must be attributed to their authors.

1. Josef Mayer’s autobiographical account

  1. Josef Mayer, “Lebenslauf + Informationsbericht”. First-person autobiographical narrative, reproduced by the Mayer Realistic website. Original composition date, manuscript location and editorial history are not yet established. The account covers his birth in Parabuc on 6 February 1881; training and work in Budapest, Vienna, Karlsbad and Nice; the development of the Realistic wave; wartime experiences; the loss of his Karlsbad business; his departure in 1948; and the founding of Mayco in Darmstadt in 1949. Its closing account reports his death on 5 January 1952. These are presented as the testimony of Mayer or his family, not automatically as independently verified records.

The autobiography gives precise dates for the Nice gold medal (11 March 1903) and the Diplôme de Professeur (2 April 1906). The original diploma or a contemporary school register has not yet been located in this collection. The surviving Mayer Realistic training certificates are different documents and should not be described as the 1906 Nice diploma.

In the same account Mayer describes the years 1938–1948, including threats, seizure of property, detention and flight. These passages are valuable testimony but are explicitly attributed to his retrospective narrative pending corroboration from official records.

2. Original patent documents

  1. Josef Mayer, “Permanent-hair-waving appliance”. United States Patent US 1,619,794, filed 19 March 1925, priority stated as 3 April 1924, granted 1 March 1927. Technical subject: a hinged, laterally applied electrical heating apparatus for hair wound on a flat curler. Complete patent PDF · Illustrated patent record · External patent record.
  2. Josef Mayer, “Hair waving method”. United States Reissue Patent US RE18,841, reissued 30 May 1933. Subject: a hair-waving method involving clamps, winding rods, protective or moisture-retaining layers and heat. Its patent lineage is distinct from US 1,619,794; it must not be described as a reissue of that appliance patent. The preserved five-page copy also contains a later disclaimer dated 1938 concerning claim 6. Complete reissue PDF · Illustrated patent record · External patent record.

These documents are reproduced on the Patents page with page images and downloadable original files. A patent is evidence of a claimed invention, not in itself proof of commercial production, priority over every competitor or the scale of adoption.

3. Contemporary trade press and the Nessler–Mayer relationship

  1. Josef Mayer’s account of an early meeting with Charles Nessler. Neue Wiener Friseur-Zeitung, no. 14, 15 July 1910, pp. 3–4, as identified in the project’s research notes. This reference requires comparison with the complete original issue before precise statements about the meeting place or sequence are published. References to salons in Frankfurt, including Hungerbüller and August Egelhofer, should not be silently merged.
  2. Hans Lehmberg, Karl Ludwig Nessler und die Erfindung der Dauerwelle. 2nd edition, published by Kadus-Haarkosmetik, Kadabell GmbH & Co. KG, Lenzkirch, 1986, pp. 86–87, section “Ehrungen – Ehrungen”. A scanned double-page spread has been examined for this project. On p. 86, Lehmberg reproduces a photograph of Charles Nessler and Josef Mayer together in Berlin and states that, on hearing Nessler was visiting Berlin in 1939, Mayer travelled there from Karlsbad to meet him. He also reports that Mayer wrote numerous letters hoping to arrange a gathering of older permanent-wave pioneers, while explicitly leaving open who attended. Lehmberg characterises the men as friends despite their commercial and technical rivalry. His comparative assessment of their achievements is Lehmberg’s interpretation, not an independent finding. The photograph’s precise photographer and reproduction rights remain unverified. Historical context and image.
  3. “Die Mayerwelle und ihre Vorzüge”. Historical German-language trade-press material represented by an image in the collection. Original periodical title, issue, date and full continuation are not yet established; research record DW-MAYER-001.

4. Photographs, certificates, advertisements and objects

Historical images supplied by REALISTIC, a.s., Karlovy Vary, Czech Republic are used with reported permission for website publication. See the Historical Documents & Visual Archive for individual reproductions and descriptions. Credits do not imply that the date, photographer, precise occasion or every person depicted has been independently established.

The French-language Mayer Realistic diplomas concern qualification in the Realistic permanent-wave system, not the Diplôme de Professeur Mayer says he received in Nice in 1906. The photograph of Nessler and Mayer in Berlin is reproduced in Lehmberg (1986), p. 86; the scan has now been examined. Attribution of the occasion to 1939 follows Lehmberg’s accompanying text. Publication of a photograph in a 1986 book does not by itself establish permission for republication online.

6. Open questions and next archival checks

  1. Locate the original 1906 Nice Diplôme de Professeur and independent documentation of the 1903 competition award.
  2. Check the full 1910 Neue Wiener Friseur-Zeitung article to establish the precise circumstances and location of Mayer’s first meeting with Nessler.
  3. Identify the photographer and original provenance of the Berlin 1939 image, and clarify republication rights. The scanned book passage (Lehmberg 1986, pp. 86–87) has been examined.
  4. Establish original publication details for undated advertisements, factory photographs and the trade article Die Mayerwelle und ihre Vorzüge.
  5. Identify surviving apparatus models against original patents, catalogues and advertisements.
  6. Where feasible, compare Mayer’s autobiographical account of 1938–1948 with contemporary administrative, court and business records.
Editorial policy. The project distinguishes “documented by a surviving original”, “reported by a contemporary participant”, “stated in a later publication” and “not yet verified”. Corrections and newly identified sources will be incorporated as the archive develops. This website is an independent research initiative, not a claim that every historical question has been settled.
