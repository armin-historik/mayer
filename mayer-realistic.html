The Josef Mayer Research Project

Mayer Realistic

A Karlsbad business connecting machinery, professional instruction and the international salon market.

The Realistic name appears on historical advertising, diplomas and exhibition material. Together these objects show a system promoted not only as a technical appliance but also as a professional identity.

Research approach. This page presents Mayer Realistic through surviving visual sources. An advertisement is evidence of what a firm publicly claimed; a diploma documents its training or promotional presentation; a later photograph documents an object but does not by itself establish its manufacture date. Where dates, model names or provenance remain uncertain, they are identified as such.

Josef Mayer, the Realistic name and the technical system

Josef Mayer is associated with the development of flat-strand permanent waving and with the commercial name Mayer Realistic in Karlsbad (today Karlovy Vary). The historical material gathered here makes it possible to study several related activities: apparatus design, equipment supply, advertising, salon services and professional presentation.

The technical basis can be examined directly in the original US patent documents held on this website. US 1,619,794 (1927) describes an electrically heated appliance whose two half-cylindrical sections open laterally around the wrapped hair. US Re. 18,841 (1933) describes a hair-waving method with flat strands, clamps and protective coverings. The latter belongs to a different patent lineage and should not be confused with a reissue of US 1,619,794.

These patents establish what Mayer disclosed and claimed. They do not automatically prove that every apparatus shown in the photographs below was built to precisely the same specification.

Business premises and production

A surviving advertisement identifies the salon of Josef Mayer on Alte Wiese in Karlsbad and advertises a factory for permanent-wave apparatuses, drying equipment and hairdressing supplies at Karlsbad-Fischern, Altrohlauerstrasse 501. The date of the advertisement and the precise identification of the factory photograph remain open.

Read together, the surviving advertisement and building photograph raise questions about the scale and organisation of the business. The address printed in the advertisement is a contemporary claim that can be checked against address books, trade directories and business records. The photograph's identification as a particular factory site remains to be independently confirmed.

What the advertisement contributes

Rather than treating the advertisement merely as decoration, it should be read as a primary source for the names, addresses and product categories used in the company's own marketing. Its wording can also help identify further archival records. Until the original publication and date are established, it should not be used to infer an exact year of production.

International presentation

The French-language display and certificates illustrate international branding. The terms Réalistic, ondulation permanente and the named Karlsbad system connect the business to francophone professional markets. The original dates and recipients of the certificates require closer inspection.

How to read the certificates

The French- and English-language diplomas provide evidence of the language and visual identity used to present the Realistic system. Their existence alone does not establish how many courses were taught, how many hairdressers were certified or in which countries particular certificates were issued. Those questions require dated, individually identifiable examples and corroborating records.

Research questions still open

The next archival steps are to establish the dates and publication venues of the surviving advertisement and diplomas, identify the exact premises represented by the factory photograph, and match surviving machines to dated product catalogues. These are research questions, not conclusions already demonstrated by the illustrations.
