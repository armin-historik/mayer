Josef Mayer: patents and original documents

Read the historical patents on this website: original drawings, complete specifications, technical interpretation and a later legal disclaimer.

Two original United States patent documents illuminate different aspects of Josef Mayer’s permanent-wave technology: an electrical heating appliance patented in 1927 and a hair-waving method reissued in 1933. The two documents must not be confused: the 1933 reissue refers to a different original patent.

Source-critical distinction. US 1,619,794 is an appliance patent. Re. 18,841 is a method reissue linked to original US 1,622,957. Both are reproduced below from the supplied historical documents.

US patent1,619,794

Granted1 March 1927
Original titlePermanent-Hair-Waving Appliance
Document3 pages, including original drawing sheet and claims

What the patent actually describes

The specification distinguishes an older method in which hair is wound helically from the roots from a method in which a flatter strand is wound from its free end toward the scalp. Mayer’s particular patented subject here is the heating appliance adapted to the latter arrangement, rather than an unrestricted claim to every form of flat winding.

The device has two semi-cylindrical electrically heated shells on scissor-like handles. The shells can be opened and placed laterally around the wrapped hair, which addresses the obstruction that would prevent a conventional sleeve heater from sliding over a flat-wound strand. The patent also describes insulating edge pieces and the electrical connections.

In the specification, a heating period of approximately 12–15 minutes is mentioned. This is a historical description, not a modern operating recommendation. The text also refers to chemical substances and electrical heating arrangements that must not be reproduced as instructions for contemporary use.

Earlier reissue statedRe. 17,398, dated 6 August 1929
AssigneeThe Philad Company, Cleveland, Ohio
1938 documentDisclaimer of claim 6 filed 20 May 1938; Official Gazette 14 June 1938

What the method document shows

The reissue describes a method using a flat strand of hair, clamps near the scalp, winding on a rod, absorbent material with treating solution, moisture-retaining layers, a protective covering and applied heat. Its drawing sheet (figures 1–6) depicts the clamp, rod, coverings and heating arrangement. The written claims define the legal subject matter; the drawings illustrate embodiments.

The fifth page in the supplied document records a later disclaimer of claim 6 by the assignee, The Philad Company. This is significant for interpreting the patent’s later legal history and is reproduced below rather than omitted.

Research notes and citation

Primary sources: Josef Mayer, Permanent-Hair-Waving Appliance, US Patent 1,619,794, granted 1 March 1927; Josef Mayer, Hair Waving Method, US Reissue Patent 18,841, reissued 30 May 1933, with disclaimer filed 20 May 1938. Reproductions from the original patent documents held in this research collection.

The 1927 specification explicitly names a Czechoslovak application dated 3 April 1924; the 1933 reissue references a German filing dated 9 April 1924. These are different statements in different documents and should not be silently merged into one priority claim.

Further study should compare these US filings with their foreign counterparts and trace the separate original patent US 1,622,957 and intermediate reissue Re. 17,398. The present page does not claim that these additional originals are already reproduced.
